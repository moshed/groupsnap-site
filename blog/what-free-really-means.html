What "free" actually gets you

Almost every event photo tool advertises a free tier. Nearly all of them cap it in the same place, and it is not where you would guess.

4 September 2026 · figures read from each vendor's own pricing page on that date

The pattern

Search for a way to collect photos from your guests and you will find a dozen products, all saying the same three things: a QR code, no app, free to start. The first two are true almost everywhere. The third is doing a lot of work.

The industry-standard free tier is about 50 uploads. Wedibox gives you 50 guest photo uploads. GuestPix's free level is similarly capped. GuestCam does not offer a free tier at all — it is $49 or nothing.

Fifty photos is not a wedding. Fifty photos is the cocktail hour. It is roughly what fifteen guests take before the speeches, and the cap therefore lands squarely in the middle of your event, which is the single worst moment to be standing in a corner entering card details on a phone.

Why capping people is a different bargain

An upload cap is designed to run out. That is its job: it is a meter, and the meter is calibrated to trip during your event, because that is when you will pay anything to make it stop.

A cap on people behaves differently. Nine people is a family trip, a birthday dinner, a hiking weekend, a set of cousins, a sheva brachos — a whole class of event that never grows past nine and therefore never pays. Those events are exactly the ones nobody would spend $49 on, so today they happen in a group chat that hands every photo back compressed and out of order.

It also means the cap is predictable. You know before the day whether your event has more than nine people in it. Nobody knows in advance how many photos their guests will take.

The honest flip side: if your event does have forty guests, GroupSnap's free tier will not carry it either. It will cost $0.99 rather than $49, but it is not free.

The two clocks nobody reads

Even on paid plans, "unlimited" is bounded by time, and by two different limits that vendors quote separately:

  • Upload window — how long guests can still add photos. Commonly 3 months on the cheapest plan, 6 on the middle one, 12 on the top.
  • Storage window — how long the gallery stays online at all. Commonly 12 to 14 months from the event.

So the default outcome across this whole category is that your wedding album disappears about a year later. That is not sharp practice — hosting costs money — but it does mean none of these products, GroupSnap included, should be the only place your photos live.

Whichever tool you pick, get the photos out. On most of these, downloading the whole gallery is part of what the paid plan is for; do it, and put the folder somewhere you control. GroupSnap includes whole-album export on the free tier — into a Photos album on your phone, or as a single .zip — but that is not a reason to skip the step. No event tool should be the only place your photos live.

So what should you use?

  • Under ten people — a free tier that caps people will genuinely stay free.
  • A wedding, and you want the trimmings — pay the $49. RSVP, guestbook and slideshow in one product is worth it, and the download at the end is the deliverable.
  • A wedding, photos only, on a budget — a per-album unlock at $0.99 does the same job for the price of nothing, as long as you back the photos up yourself.
  • Anything you must keep — no event tool. Get it into your own storage.
